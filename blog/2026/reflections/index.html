march 28, 2026

Welcome to Day 0. I worry about AI’s effects on my cognitive ability - specifically writing, coding, doing math etc. Addmitedly, I got caught in the deafening hype/discourse on claude code and tried it for my own research. Surprisingly, it kind of worked and abosolutely smashed through multiple tasks I gave it. Until it didn’t… Throughout this whole process however, what I did feel and continue to feel is a kind of awful brain-fog that I have scarcely dealt with before. I’ve seen Andrej Karpathy and the lot use different terms for this. AI-induced pyschosis is one of them. Im not even going to begin to address whether these terms are accurate but rather simply say, rather in theme, that they kind of capture the overall “vibe” of what people who use AI for knowledge-work and heavy intellectual tasks feel. Its almost certainly mischaracterized in my case, because people who use this phrase almost describe a kind of uncontrallable ability to be productive and build things. But it would be unscientific not to seperate the effects of AI with other circumstances in my life. I just got into multiple PhD programs and I’m dealing wiht a kind of post-decision fatigue/emptiness which often comes when a long-term goal is achieved. Finally, combine this with the effects of algorithms (social media) etc. and its a perfect storm. In the next few weeks, I will document my efforts to overcome this temporary state I am stuck in. In the past, whats the helped the most is a dedicated medidation practise, some sort of gratitude exercise (im not sure whether I want to expand on this), reading etc. AI presents a unique challenge though because it allows a unique level of congnitive malaise. While for research, I almost have to continue to use AI (especially for things like loading experimental data, making plots etc) I’m gonna try a few things - one idea from a friend is to let it leave chunks of code for you to fill out. Let’s see what works and what doesn’t, all in due time.

march 30, 2026

Day 2. Cringe musings and reflections from yesterday aside, I’ve decided I do want to share some more personal opinions. I’ll admit, I’ve been thinking plenty about what a future with AGI looks like and its consumed a good (euphemism for unhealthy) amount of my time recently. I’ll also admit, no amount of thinking has led me to think this future looks particularly rosy. First of all, my knowledge is limited especially when it comes to LLMs. But I do use these boiled-down productized versions of these systems and have used them long enough to note their improvement. RAPID IMPROVEMENT. I have always been of the position that the AI industry isn’t a bubble in a conventional sense. The kind of investment, improvement, “hype” etc. we see is not akin to some new technological product or paradigm. Its more akin to leaps in technology which fundamentally change the nature of power - think more manhattan project. Its the only way to justify the level of investment we are seeing - some things transcend economic/monetary returns and i hope people realize that. Was racing to the moon economically productive? Perhaps, but it doesn’t deliver immediate returns in the way you would expect and perhaps ever, especially if you look at the fact that the last Apollo mission was what ? 50-60 years ago?. So unpreccedented investments in technology for no CLEAR immediate-term monetary returns have been done before and will continue to happen. So whats the currency at stake here with AI? Well, I alluded to it earlier and you can read between lines xD. Then, after all this, the immediate issue in my own life continues to remain. AI doom or hype really doesn’t matter if you have no agency/energy to enact positive change. You will just drive yourself crazy. In my case, consuming 1 hour of Yoshua Bengio interviews after lunch was a waste of time, not because Bengio isn’t amazing but because its misplaced time in my own life. I think the window to have positive impact is quickly closing and for me, I find myself lucky enough to have a PhD offer from an amazing school! Being a defeatist achieves nothing and so if there is any motivation for me, its that I have this oppurtunity in front of me and if I channel all my energy towards this and see what happens. Reminding myself of this is really the best thing I can do, because often thats what mobilizes me and has done in the past. And the fact that - we are all in this together.

Oh - another thought I think I should expand on. I saw a couple of videos on Youtube where people were reminiscing a time where they so smart and AI has dumbed down and ruined the art of coding for them etc. I, ahem, am definitely not guilty of thinking of things this way (don’t scroll up pls). Point is, I personally find myself doing this often or at least I used to. We look up to idealised versions of our past and dream about idealised versions of what our future should like. Of course, thats fine. Thats human. However, one of the most powerful things thats helped me in my life is sort of eliminating that nagging thought of regret which tugs you to moments in time which are either past you or simply fictional. It took me an inordinate amount of time to get there, and frankly, when I say I got there I more mean I’m semi-there. My point still stands and one of the most beautiful things I’ve realized is you can truly change your inner loops of thoughts in truly meaningful ways - change is possible and far more realisable than we think. My biggest moments of change have not been consistent marches of 12+ months but rather shorter bursts of 2-3 months of intense thoughts, practise, work etc. I’ve realized after those periods while you might regress to the so-called mean, you’ll leave artifacts of change (almost like .md files) which permantly but subtly alter your way of thinking. I’m also a big believer in the growth mindset. What I mean is that our brains are more plastic than we realise and better still, believing we are more plastic probably aids in our ability to learn more and enact change. Thats why belief is so important - its sort of this inner contextual guidance system that can be hacked easily but is so so potent :D. For instance, I used to have a very small dose of Melatnonin (which is OTC btw) to help me sleep every night for a few months this summer for whatever reason. The same applies to social media. The cost of social media usage is its ability to affect how you function cognitively. Who knows, maybe 20 years from now we’ll find out that excessive social media use is strongly correlated with early-onset dementia etc (this is pure conjecture, but my point still stands). At the very minimum, you life a less rich life because your mental space is consumed by garbage material which propogate terrible societal beliefs and enable people to hide behind a shield of anonymity. Day 26th. Its my birthday!!!!!!! Yay! Putting that aside, I’ve been feeling extreme levels of boredom everyday. But I’d argue thats almost a good thing. Boredom is a catalyst for great things generally. Boredom is also almost a privilege nowadays - many people have many responsibilities and pressing things to do and really crave some time to themselves. Worst still, social media, news, TV and even worse things have occupied every non-busy moment in our lives and this hyper stimulation has, for lack of better words, fried our brains. Apparently, there was a time in history (in the UK) where doctors felt that boredom was a scourge and was tormenting society. Now its the opposite. Boredom is prescribed. Its a scarce resource. Its a cure for the seemingly incurable desire to be stimulated 24/7. Yet, in a world where the price of cheap entertainment has essentially hit 0, our attention-deprived brains naturally take the path of least resistance. How can you blame us, after all? Well, among the other things I’ve tried, I want to embrace boredom. In the few days of me doing so, I think its already led to small wins. My hunger to learn, to create interesting things and my long-lost creativity are finally coming back but I’ve been so used to being busy or stimulated constantly that its almost agonizing to have to deal with it. LET THE BOREDOM MOVEMENT CONTINUE.

Day 29th. The minute you open instagram or linkedin, you are exposed to reels, stories, posts, highlights of people’s lives. No doubt, you also see “tips”, “advice” and the infamous, “heres what a dinner with friends taught me about B2BSAAS” (this was an actual post). Guys. What the fuck are we doing here. This isn’t healthy. We’ve hyper-sensationalized, hyper-compactified, hyper-gamified every FUCKING aspect of our lives. I can’t help but feel this sucks out meaning in what we do on a day to day basis because we are always chasing this superior state of productivity, connectedness or whatever the fuck we are after. Your “lived experience” is computed by your own thoughts and experiences - if you take away what you want and what others do, whats left? I’d argue, the most important part. Because at the end of day, you have to live with yourself, in your own head, which is constantly swirling with hopefully intersting thoughts, curiosities, desires etc. I think we should all try to boil our experience in life to its most irreducible, purest parts which is where we can think for ourselves, have interests unpurturbed by some expecation of reward or performance. Social media can, and often does, add extra layers of junk to this irreducible core of your mind till its essentially unrecognizable. Congratulations, you are now a homogenized lump of others interests and worse still, others definition of success. MAXIMUM ENTROPY. Happiness, contentness lies in purity of thought which I’d argue occurs when your mind can take all the information around you without the bagguage it usually carries. —
