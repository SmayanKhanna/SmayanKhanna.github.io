## august 18, 2026

The last time I did a similar exercise (regrettably in April), I felt that it had a profound impact on me. Documenting my thoughts, albiet publicly, was a very useful exercise for me. So why am I back? Well, my first announcement is that I can ... well, finally announce that I'll be going to UCSD for my PhD (in Data Science). I have my own grievances about doing a "Data Science" PhD, not because of the program or my research interests but more so the name of the field itself. Data Science is slightly irksome because to me, it neither fits within the new-ish (I said it) but arguably more prestigious field of Computer Science and the more rigorous, hardcore field of Statistics. It is also somewhat akin to a buzzword in Linkedin influencer circles (on similar lines as LLMB2BSAASAI). Realistically, however, no one cares and no one should care because your research, PI and own skills matter x1000 more than the name of your PhD program. Nevertheless, 4 years of physics has indoctrinated me into becoming an elitist (which is stupid by the way, don't be an elitist). In preparation for the PhD I find myself (again) in a position where my brain is fried. To recover my attention span, I intend on using the Pomodoro technique to really quite benchmark just how much I can focus. In short, I will benchmark how many 30 minutes Pomodoro sessions I can fit in a day and how I feel roughly during each one. At present, towards the end of the day, even cramming 1 session in feels excrutiatingly difficult. Nevertheless, your attention span and the ability to do hard, cognitively hard things, is a muscle. Lets see what happens.
